## 1. Theory Refresher (≈ 150 words)

Linear regression models the relationship between a **scalar response** \(y\) and one or more **predictors** \(\mathbf{x} = (x_1,\dots,x_p)^\top\) by assuming a linear form  

\[
y = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_1 + \dots + \beta_p x_p + \varepsilon,
\qquad \varepsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2).
\]

In matrix notation for \(n\) observations  

\[
\mathbf{y} = \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon},
\qquad 
\mathbf{X} = 
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & x_{11} & \dots & x_{1p}\\
1 & x_{21} & \dots & x_{2p}\\
\vdots & \vdots & & \vdots\\
1 & x_{n1} & \dots & x_{np}
\end{bmatrix},
\quad
\boldsymbol{\beta} = (\beta_0,\beta_1,\dots,\beta_p)^\top .
\]

The **ordinary least‑squares (OLS)** estimator minimizes the residual sum of squares (RSS)

\[
\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{OLS}} = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta}} \|\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^{2}
        = (\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{y},
\]

provided \(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}\) is invertible.  

**Regularization** adds a penalty to control variance:

* **Ridge** (ℓ₂): \(\displaystyle \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{ridge}} = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta}} \|\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^{2} + \lambda\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^{2}\)  
  → closed‑form \((\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X}+\lambda\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{y}\).

* **Lasso** (ℓ₁): \(\displaystyle \hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}_{\text{lasso}} = \arg\min_{\boldsymbol{\beta}} \|\mathbf{y} - \mathbf{X}\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_2^{2} + \lambda\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\|_1\)  
  → solved by coordinate descent; yields sparse solutions.

Model assessment typically uses **\(R^2\)**, **Adjusted \(R^2\)**, **RMSE**, and **cross‑validation**.

## 3. Problem 1 – Simple Linear Regression (Closed‑form)

### Scenario
A researcher measures the concentration of a pollutant (µg L⁻¹) in a river (response \(y\)) and the distance downstream from a factory (km) (predictor \(x\)). The data for 8 sites are:

| i | \(x_i\) (km) | \(y_i\) (µg L⁻¹) |
|---|--------------|-----------------|
| 1 | 0.5 | 12.1 |
| 2 | 1.0 | 11.3 |
| 3 | 1.5 | 10.8 |
| 4 | 2.0 | 9.7 |
| 5 | 2.5 | 9.2 |
| 6 | 3.0 | 8.4 |
| 7 | 3.5 | 7.9 |
| 8 | 4.0 | 7.1 |

Assume the classical linear model \(y_i = \beta_0 + \beta_1 x_i + \varepsilon_i\) with i.i.d. \(\varepsilon_i\sim N(0,\sigma^2)\).

### Questions
1. **(a)** Derive the OLS estimates \(\hat\beta_0,\hat\beta_1\) using the normal equations.  
2. **(b)** Compute the residual standard error \(\hat\sigma\).  
3. **(c)** Construct a 95 % confidence interval for \(\beta_1\).  
4. **(d)** Predict the pollutant concentration at \(x = 5\) km and give a 95 % prediction interval.

### Hints
* Use the formulas  
  \[
  \hat\beta_1 = \frac{\sum (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y)}{\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2},\qquad
  \hat\beta_0 = \bar y - \hat\beta_1\bar x .
  \]
* Residual variance estimator: \(\displaystyle \hat\sigma^2 = \frac{1}{n-2}\sum (y_i-\hat y_i)^2\).  
* For a simple regression, \(\operatorname{Var}(\hat\beta_1)=\hat\sigma^2 / \sum (x_i-\bar x)^2\).  
* Prediction interval at \(x_0\):  
  \[
  \hat y_0 \pm t_{n-2,0.975}\,\hat\sigma\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{n}+\frac{(x_0-\bar x)^2}{\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2}} .
  \]

### Solution

#### (a) Compute sample means
\[
\bar x = \frac{0.5+1+1.5+2+2.5+3+3.5+4}{8}=2.0\;\text{km},
\qquad
\bar y = \frac{12.1+11.3+10.8+9.7+9.2+8.4+7.9+7.1}{8}=9.675.
\]

#### (b) Numerators & denominators
\[
\begin{aligned}
\sum (x_i-\bar x)(y_i-\bar y) &= 
(0.5-2)(12.1-9.675) + \dots + (4-2)(7.1-9.675)\\
&= (-1.5)(2.425) + (-1)(1.625) + (-0.5)(1.125) + (0)(0.025)\\
&\quad + (0.5)(-0.475) + (1)(-1.275) + (1.5)(-1.775) + (2)(-2.575)\\
&= -3.6375 -1.625 -0.5625 +0 -0.2375 -1.275 -2.6625 -5.15\\
&= -15.15.
\end{aligned}
\]

\[
\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2 = (-1.5)^2+(-1)^2+(-0.5)^2+0^2+0.5^2+1^2+1.5^2+2^2 = 10.0.
\]

Hence  

\[
\boxed{\hat\beta_1 = \frac{-15.15}{10}= -1.515},\qquad
\boxed{\hat\beta_0 = \bar y - \hat\beta_1\bar x = 9.675 - (-1.515)(2)=12.705 }.
\]

The fitted line: \(\hat y = 12.705 - 1.515x\).

#### (c) Residuals & \(\hat\sigma\)

Compute fitted values \(\hat y_i\) and residuals \(e_i = y_i-\hat y_i\):

| i | \(x_i\) | \(y_i\) | \(\hat y_i\) = 12.705‑1.515\(x_i\) | \(e_i\) |
|---|--------|--------|-----------------------------------|--------|
|1|0.5|12.1|12.705‑0.7575=11.9475|0.1525|
|2|1.0|11.3|11.190|0.110|
|3|1.5|10.8|9.4325|1.3675|
|4|2.0|9.7|8.675|1.025|
|5|2.5|9.2|7.9175|1.2825|
|6|3.0|8.4|7.16|1.240|
|7|3.5|7.9|6.4025|1.4975|
|8|4.0|7.1|5.645|1.455|

\[
\sum e_i^2 = 0.1525^2+0.110^2+1.3675^2+1.025^2+1.2825^2+1.240^2+1.4975^2+1.455^2 \approx 9.78.
\]

Degrees of freedom \(= n-2 = 6\).  

\[
\boxed{\hat\sigma^2 = \frac{9.78}{6}=1.63},\qquad
\boxed{\hat\sigma = \sqrt{1.63}=1.28}.
\]

#### (d) Confidence interval for \(\beta_1\)

\[
\operatorname{Var}(\hat\beta_1)=\frac{\hat\sigma^2}{\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2}= \frac{1.63}{10}=0.163.
\]
Standard error \(SE(\hat\beta_1)=\sqrt{0.163}=0.404\).

Critical value \(t_{6,0.975}=2.447\) (from t‑table).  

\[
\boxed{ \beta_1 \in \hat\beta_1 \pm t\,SE = -1.515 \pm 2.447(0.404) } 
\Longrightarrow (-2.51,\,-0.52).
\]

Interpretation: With 95 % confidence the true slope lies between –2.51 and –0.52 µg L⁻¹ per km.

#### (e) Prediction at \(x_0=5\) km

\[
\hat y_0 = 12.705 - 1.515(5)=5.130.
\]

Standard error of prediction:

\[
\begin{aligned}
SE_{\text{pred}} &= \hat\sigma\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{n}+\frac{(x_0-\bar x)^2}{\sum (x_i-\bar x)^2}}\\
&=1.28\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{8}+\frac{(5-2)^2}{10}}\\
&=1.28\sqrt{1+0.125+0.9}=1.28\sqrt{2.025}=1.28(1.423)=1.82.
\end{aligned}
\]

Prediction interval:

\[
5.130 \pm 2.447 \times 1.82 \; \Rightarrow\; 5.130 \pm 4.45 = (0.68,\;9.58).
\]

So we expect the pollutant concentration at 5 km to be **≈ 5.1 µg L⁻¹**, with a 95 % prediction interval of **0.7 – 9.6 µg L⁻¹**.

## 4. Problem 2 – Multiple Linear Regression (Matrix Form)

### Scenario
A data scientist is modeling house prices (in \$10 000) using three predictors:

* \(x_1\): square footage (in 100 ft²)  
* \(x_2\): age of the house (years)  
* \(x_3\): binary indicator for presence of a garage (1 = yes, 0 = no)

The training set contains \(n=5\) houses:

| House | \(x_1\) | \(x_2\) | \(x_3\) | \(y\) |
|-------|--------|--------|--------|------|
| 1 | 2.5 | 10 | 1 | 30 |
| 2 | 3.0 | 5  | 0 | 35 |
| 3 | 1.8 | 20 | 1 | 22 |
| 4 | 2.2 | 15 | 0 | 28 |
| 5 | 3.5 | 8  | 1 | 40 |

### Questions
1. **(a)** Write the design matrix \(\mathbf{X}\) and response vector \(\mathbf{y}\).  
2. **(b)** Compute the OLS coefficient vector \(\hat{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\) using the matrix formula. Show each intermediate matrix product.  
3. **(c)** Interpret the estimated coefficient for the garage variable.  
4. **(d)** Predict the price of a new house with \((x_1,x_2,x_3) = (2.8,12,1)\).  

### Hints
* Augment \(\mathbf{X}\) with a column of ones for the intercept.  
* Use a calculator or symbolic software for \((\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{X})^{-1}\).  
* Interpretation: holding other variables constant, the coefficient tells the marginal effect of adding a garage.

\begin{bmatrix}

\end{bmatrix}.
\]

\]

2. **\(\mathbf{X}^\top\mathbf{y}\)**  

\[
